## 2. The five agents and their responsibilities

These are five distinct responsibilities, not five independent autonomous bots. Every agent produces structured output that the orchestrator can validate.

1\. Scout — Opportunity Hunter

Finds potential opportunities and problems.

- Detects unresolved inquiries and abandoned sales conversations.
- Identifies best-selling and slow-moving products.
- Flags stockout risks, sales declines, and changes in order patterns.
- Identifies repeat customers and potential cross-selling opportunities.
- Attaches evidence references, date ranges, and confidence limitations.

Output: ranked opportunities, supporting evidence, missing data, and suggested investigations.

2\. Muse — Campaign Creator

Turns validated opportunities into actionable marketing proposals.

- Creates campaign concepts, captions, offers, and creative briefs.
- Suggests bundles and cross-sells using available products.
- Reuses existing Smart Marketing and poster-generation functionality.
- Creates campaign variants for controlled experiments.
- Must use verified product names, prices, stock availability, and offer rules.

Output: campaign proposal, target segment, creative assets or briefs, budget assumptions, and success metric.

3\. Guardian — Profit Protector

Protects the business from financially unsafe recommendations.

- Calculates unit economics and contribution margins.
- Evaluates discounts, bundles, delivery costs, and campaign spending.
- Enforces minimum-margin and discount policies.
- Flags missing costs, uncertain attribution, and misleading forecasts.
- Can veto or require revision of proposals that violate policy.

Output: calculations, assumptions, policy violations, allowed offer limits, and warnings.

4\. Echo — Customer Advocate

Investigates customer friction using actual interactions.

- Classifies explicit objections such as price, delivery, availability, and payment.
- Detects unanswered inquiries and where conversations stop.
- Compares conversation outcomes with completed orders.
- Suggests improvements to sales scripts and FAQ content.
- Distinguishes observed behavior from inferred reasons.

Output: objection categories, counts, examples, evidence references, and suggested improvements.

5\. Commander — Strategy Lead

Combines the findings into one coherent decision.

- Prioritizes opportunities by impact, confidence, effort, and risk.
- Compares conflicting proposals.
- Rejects unsupported numerical claims.
- Selects the best-supported plan—or explicitly recommends collecting more data.
- Produces a decision card with actions, expected outcomes, limitations, and approval requirements.

Output: a prioritized strategy, alternatives, trade-offs, and an owner approval request.

### Agent interaction rules

1. Scout and Echo investigate independently where useful.
2. Guardian calculates financial constraints from trusted business data.
3. Muse proposes campaigns using the opportunity and constraints.
4. Guardian reviews each proposal; Muse revises proposals that violate constraints.
5. Commander compares the surviving proposals and creates the final decision.
6. A validation service checks every evidence reference and required field.
7. Only the owner-approved, validated action can enter the execution system.

Keep this as a bounded workflow, not an endless conversation. Set maximum agent iterations, model-call budgets, timeouts, and retry rules.

Use UUID primary keys, foreign keys, `created_at`/`updated_at`, indexes on common lookup fields, and database constraints for status values. For tables containing tenant-owned records, include `shop_id` and use composite foreign keys where practical to prevent cross-shop references.

## 5. Analytics and business rules

This is one of the most important parts of the product. If the analytics are wrong, a more advanced model will only produce more convincing mistakes.

### 5.1 Build a deterministic analytics layer first

Create reusable SQL functions or views for:

- Daily and weekly gross sales.
- Completed, cancelled, and refunded orders.
- Average order value.
- Product sales and stock movement.
- Inquiry-to-order conversion.
- Unresolved inquiries by age.
- Repeat-customer rate, where customer identity is reliable.
- Campaign-attributed orders and revenue.
- Estimated contribution margin.
- Stockout and low-stock risk.
- Data freshness and coverage.

Use the existing source tables. Define which order statuses count as revenue and how refunds, cancellations, delivery charges, and discounts are treated. Do not silently equate order value with collected cash or profit.

### 5.2 Essential formulas

Average order value

\\[ AOV = \frac{\text{Net sales value}}{\text{Number of eligible completed orders}} \\]

Contribution per order

\\[ \text{Contribution} = \text{Net revenue} -\text{Product costs} -\text{Variable delivery costs} -\text{Other variable costs} \\]

Contribution margin percentage

\\[ \text{Margin \\%} = \frac{\text{Contribution}}{\text{Net revenue}}\times100 \\]

If product costs or other required costs are missing, return `unknown` for the affected calculation. Never substitute zero and claim the business is profitable.

Inquiry conversion rate

\\[ \text{Conversion rate} = \frac{\text{Eligible inquiries linked to completed orders}} {\text{Eligible inquiries}}\times100 \\]

Use a defined attribution window and avoid counting one customer or order multiple times. Report unmatched orders and attribution uncertainty separately.

### 5.3 Opportunity scoring

Use a transparent score to help rank opportunities, not to pretend that future outcomes are certain.

For a first version, normalize four dimensions to a 0–100 scale:

- Impact: likely business importance.
- Confidence: strength and completeness of evidence.
- Urgency: how soon action is required.
- Feasibility: ability to act with available resources.

A starting score:

\\[ S = 0.40I + 0.30C + 0.15U + 0.15F \\]

Here, \\(I,C,U,F\\) are normalized scores for impact, confidence, urgency, and feasibility. The weights are initial product assumptions; calibrate them against owner feedback and measured outcomes.

Add hard gates:

- Missing critical evidence → label as needs investigation.
- Violates business policy → block the proposed action.
- Insufficient data → lower confidence and explain why.
- Duplicate active opportunity → merge or suppress.
- Low-impact, high-effort recommendation → deprioritize.

Store the component scores and explanations. The model can describe the opportunity, but deterministic code should calculate the final score.

### 5.4 Stockout risk

Estimate stockout risk only if the available inventory and sales history support it.

A simple first model:

\\[ \text{Estimated days of stock} = \frac{\text{Current available stock}} {\text{Average daily units sold}} \\]

Compare estimated days of stock against supplier lead time and a safety-stock policy. If the sales history is too short, intermittent, or missing, show insufficient evidence instead of a precise risk probability.

### 5.5 Analytics API contract

Every metric should return both a value and the context needed to interpret it.

```
type MetricResult = {
  metricKey: string;
  value: number | null;
  unit: string;
  periodStart: string;
  periodEnd: string;
  calculatedAt: string;
  coverage: number | null;
  sourceCount: number;
  limitations: string[];
  formulaVersion: string;
};
```

Store formula versions so that later changes in business definitions do not silently rewrite the meaning of old decisions.

## 6. AI contracts and evidence validation

Never allow each agent to return arbitrary prose that downstream code must guess how to interpret.

Use structured outputs and validate them at runtime with a schema library such as Zod.

### 6.1 Proposal contract

```
type AgentProposal = {
  agent: "scout" | "muse" | "guardian" | "echo";
  title: string;
  hypothesis: string;
  evidenceRefs: string[];
  assumptions: string[];
  estimatedImpact: {
    metric: string;
    low?: number;
    high?: number;
    unit: string;
    basis: "observed" | "scenario" | "forecast";
  };
  risks: string[];
  missingData: string[];
  recommendedAction: string;
  requiresOwnerApproval: true;
};
```

Validation rules:

1. Every `evidenceRef` must resolve to an authorized record from the current shop and session.
2. Numeric claims must match stored analytics or be explicitly labeled as assumptions or forecasts.
3. Forecasts must state the assumptions and measurement period.
4. No profitability claims when required cost inputs are unknown.
5. A recommendation must identify risks and missing data.
6. Model-generated action descriptions must never be treated as executable instructions.

### 6.2 Final decision contract

```

  primaryProposalId: string | null;
  alternatives: Array<{
    proposalId: string;
    reason: string;
    tradeoffs: string[];
  }>;
  opportunityScore: number | null;
  scoreBreakdown: {
    impact: number;
    confidence: number;
    urgency: number;
    feasibility: number;
  } | null;
  evidenceRefs: string[];
  assumptions: string[];
  risks: string[];
  missingData: string[];
  nextActions: Array<{
    type:
      | "draft_follow_up"
      | "draft_campaign"
      | "create_bundle"
      | "inventory_review"
      | "collect_more_data";
    description: string;
    requiresApproval: true;
  }>;
  status: "ready_for_review" | "needs_more_data";
};
```

In production, validate enum values, numeric ranges, field lengths, and references. The `nextActions` structure describes permitted action types; the executor must still construct a trusted action payload from server-validated records.

### 6.3 Prevent fabricated evidence

Use a separate validation service between the model and the user interface.

- Check that evidence IDs exist and belong to the current shop.
- Verify metrics against database results.
- Detect stale product prices, inventory, and delivery rates.
- Reject malformed outputs and retry once with schema errors.
- If validation fails again, return a transparent failure or partial result.
- Record model, prompt version, schema version, and validation outcome.

The UI should distinguish observed fact, AI interpretation, scenario estimate, and forecast visually and in text.

### 7.2 Session creation

The frontend submits a goal such as:

> Find ways to recover lost sales and improve profit over the last 30 days.

The backend should:

1. Authenticate the user.
2. Resolve the shop from trusted membership records.
3. Validate the goal and requested date range.
4. Load current shop policies and available analytics.
5. Create a persistent `team_sessions` record.
6. Queue or start the workflow.
7. Return the session ID and current status.

Do not let a frontend user supply an arbitrary `shop_id` and rely on that value for authorization.

### 7.3 Agent runner interface

Use one shared agent runner with different configurations.

```
type AgentContext = {
  sessionId: string;
  shopId: string;
  dateRange: {
    start: string;
    end: string;
  };
  evidenceRefs: string[];
  policySnapshot: Record<string, unknown>;
};

interface SalesAgent<TOutput> {
  readonly key:
    | "scout"
    | "muse"
    | "guardian"
    | "echo"
    | "commander";

  run(context: AgentContext): Promise<TOutput>;
}
```

Implement the shared runner with these responsibilities:

- Load only permitted evidence.
- Build the role-specific prompt.
- Call Gemini server-side.
- Parse structured output.
- Validate the output.
- Persist the run and usage.
- Return a typed result or a typed failure.

Guardian should use deterministic calculations for financial checks. The LLM may explain the calculations, but it must not override them.

### 7.4 Reliable background processing

Edge Functions can time out or be retried, so do not assume a single request will complete an entire council session.

- Persist every stage and its result.
- Make agent runs resumable.
- Use a queue or database-backed job runner with a lease/claim mechanism.
- Use unique stage keys to prevent duplicate work.
- Store retry count, last error, and next-attempt time.
- Make each transition atomic where practical.
- Distinguish transient errors from permanent validation failures.
- Resume interrupted sessions without repeating completed work.

For a first implementation, a Postgres-backed job table and a worker function are sufficient if concurrency and claiming are handled correctly. Introduce a more elaborate queue only if actual workload requires it.

## 9. Approval, execution, and safety

This layer must be built before automated execution is enabled.

### 9.1 Separate proposals from executable actions

An AI proposal is untrusted input. It is not an instruction for the backend to execute.

The safe flow is:

1. The model proposes an action.
2. The backend validates its type and fields.
3. The owner sees a preview.
4. The owner approves a specific version of the action.
5. The backend records the approval and a hash of the approved payload.
6. The executor reloads current data and validates the action again.
7. The action is executed once, with retry protection.
8. The result is written to an audit log.

If a product price, stock level, recipient list, or campaign changes after approval, invalidate the approval or request confirmation again for the changed payload.

### 9.2 Idempotent execution

Each executable action needs a unique idempotency key. Retrying a job must not send the same campaign or follow-up twice.

```
type ApprovedAction = {
  id: string;
  shopId: string;
  decisionId: string;
  actionType:
    | "telegram_follow_up"
    | "marketing_campaign";
  payload: Record<string, unknown>;
  payloadHash: string;
  approvedBy: string;
  approvedAt: string;
  idempotencyKey: string;
};
```

The server must derive `approvedBy` from the authenticated approval record, not trust it from the frontend. Restrict action types to an allowlist and validate each payload with a separate schema.

### 9.3 Telegram-specific safeguards

For inquiry recovery:

- Use actual inquiry timestamps and conversation state.
- Exclude customers who already ordered, are actively chatting, or have opted out, where those states are available.
- Apply shop-configured follow-up limits and a cooldown.
- Generate a draft before sending.
- Let the owner inspect the recipient and exact message.
- Use a unique action key to prevent duplicate delivery attempts.
- Record send success, delivery status if available, replies, and attributed orders.
- Never claim a message was delivered merely because the API accepted the request.

For marketing:

- Validate the offer against Guardian's financial constraints.
- Recheck product availability and price.
- Require approval of the exact audience, copy, and offer.
- Track the resulting campaign through existing marketing services.

### 9.4 Security checklist

- RLS on every tenant-owned table.
- Verify shop membership in every privileged Edge Function.
- No service-role key or Gemini key in frontend bundles.
- Restrict agent data access by role.
- Prevent cross-shop evidence references.
- Treat customer messages and product descriptions as untrusted content, not instructions.
- Keep prompts separate from retrieved user data.
- Apply rate limits, token budgets, and maximum session costs.
- Audit approvals, rejections, edits, and execution results.
- Avoid storing unnecessary personally identifiable information in prompts or AI logs.

## 10. Campaign attribution and outcome tracking

This is what turns Sale Brain from a recommendation generator into a system that can learn which decisions actually work.

### 10.1 Add campaign-to-sale tracking

Extend the existing campaign model rather than creating a parallel marketing system.

Capture, where supported:

- Campaign ID and version.
- Target audience or eligible recipient set.
- Send attempts and successful sends.
- Clicks or tracked visits.
- Resulting inquiries.
- Orders linked to the campaign.
- Revenue, refunds, discounts, and contribution.
- Attribution method and uncertainty.
- Start and end times.

For Telegram, campaign-specific links or codes may help connect a campaign to later inquiries and orders. For customers who order without a tracked link or code, classify the relationship as unmatched or uncertain rather than claiming attribution.

### 10.2 Measure outcomes correctly

Distinguish these measures:

| Measure            | What it tells you                                                  |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Sent messages      | Execution volume                                                   |
| Reply rate         | Customer engagement                                                |
| Inquiry conversion | Sales funnel effectiveness                                         |
| Attributed revenue | Revenue linked under a stated attribution rule                     |
| Contribution       | Financial return after defined variable costs                      |
| Incremental lift   | Estimated improvement compared with a suitable baseline or control |

Attributed revenue is not automatically incremental revenue. Some customers would have purchased anyway.

### 10.3 Controlled experiments

When feasible, use a randomized holdout group to compare a new strategy with business-as-usual. Randomization must respect consent, shop policy, and customer communication limits.

For smaller shops without enough data, use simpler before/after comparisons, clearly label their limitations, and avoid strong causal claims.

Record the hypothesis, selected metric, measurement window, audience rules, and analysis method before launching the experiment. This reduces the temptation to choose a favorable metric after seeing the results.

## 11. Business Memory and learning

Do not train a new model on every interaction. Initially, learning should mean reusing verified business knowledge and measured outcomes.

### Memory categories

Policies and preferences

Owner-confirmed rules such as discount limits, preferred tone, and approval requirements.

Verified facts

Confirmed product details, business context, and validated operating constraints.

Experiment results

Which tested campaign or recovery strategy performed well under stated conditions.

Corrections

Owner corrections to classifications or recommendations, stored with their source and scope.

For every memory item, store its source, verification status, timestamp, scope, and expiry if applicable. A campaign result should not be generalized to every product, season, or customer segment.

At the start of a council session, retrieve only relevant memory. Do not send the entire history of every decision to Gemini.

## 13. Detailed implementation phases

- Map every planned integration to an existing function or a new module.

Acceptance criteria

- A domain map identifies the existing source of truth for each business entity.
- A schema-gap report lists exactly what needs to change.
- No duplicate product, order, or campaign system is introduced.

### Phase 1 — Data foundation and analytics

Build the trustworthy evidence layer

Acceptance criteria

- Metric results match independently calculated test fixtures.

### Phase 2 — Agent framework and Sales Council

First complete AI workflow

Tasks

- Define typed agent interfaces and JSON schemas.

Acceptance criteria

- A session can complete from investigation to a validated decision.
- Every material numerical claim is verifiable or explicitly labeled as an estimate.

- A session can finish with “insufficient evidence” rather than inventing a strategy.

### Phase 3 — Team Room and Sales War Room

Make the system understandable to nontechnical owners

Tasks

Acceptance criteria

- No external action runs without a valid owner approval.
- Repeated job attempts do not duplicate an action.

- Build experiment outcome records and reporting.

Acceptance criteria

- Attributed and unattributed orders are distinguished.
- Outcomes can be traced to the campaign and approved decision.
- The reporting never treats attribution as proof of incremental sales.

### Phase 6 — Business Memory

Build the shop-specific knowledge advantage

Tasks

- Store owner-confirmed policies and preferences.
- Store verified decision and experiment outcomes.
- Add source references, expiry, corrections, and deletion.
- Retrieve only relevant memory for each session.
- Prevent unverified AI conclusions from becoming permanent facts.

Acceptance criteria

- The council uses relevant past decisions.
- Owners can inspect and correct memory.
- Expired or deleted memory is not silently reused.

### Phase 7 — Evaluation and production hardening

Prove reliability and value

Tasks

- Build a fixed test set of representative shop scenarios.
- Compare council results with the current single-agent baseline.
- Test authorization, prompt injection, stale data, duplicate execution, and failure recovery.
- Add usage limits, cost monitoring, structured logs, and alerting.
- Pilot with a small group of consenting shops before broad rollout.

Acceptance criteria

- No unresolved critical authorization or duplicate-action defects.
- Financial calculations pass deterministic tests.
- Evidence accuracy and execution reliability meet agreed release thresholds.
- The product provides useful results without requiring owners to trust unsupported claims.

## 14. Testing strategy

Treat testing as a core feature, not a final cleanup task.

### Unit tests

Test:

- Revenue and contribution calculations.
- Order status eligibility.
- Opportunity scoring.
- Financial policy constraints.
- Proposal and decision schemas.
- Evidence-reference resolution.
- Idempotency-key generation.
- Attribution rules and outcome calculations.

### Integration tests

Use a test shop with seeded products, orders, inventory, conversations, and campaigns.

Verify:

- The council uses the correct shop's records.
- Scout and Echo produce evidence-linked findings.
- Guardian blocks an offer below the configured margin threshold.
- Commander can choose “collect more data.”
- The owner can approve, edit, reject, and cancel proposals.
- An approved action is executed once.
- A failed worker resumes without repeating successful work.
- A completed order is linked to the appropriate experiment only under the defined attribution rule.

### Adversarial tests

Include:

- A customer message that tells the AI to reveal secrets or ignore its role.
- A product description containing malicious instructions.
- A forged evidence ID from another shop.
- An expired product price or unavailable item.
- Missing product costs.
- Contradictory metrics from incomplete source data.
- A stale approval payload.
- Repeated execution requests and simultaneous approval attempts.

### Evaluation dataset

Build a versioned set of representative cases: healthy shop, declining sales, insufficient history, stockout risk, missing costs, unanswered inquiries, failed campaign, and misleading AI-generated claims.

Evaluate agent outputs against known evidence and expected behavior. Track regressions when changing prompts, model versions, or schemas.

## 15. Performance, cost, and observability

A coordinated AI system can become expensive if every agent reads every record and debates every other agent. Control this from the beginning.

### Cost-control rules

- Aggregate data in SQL before sending it to Gemini.
- Fetch only the evidence relevant to the investigation.
- Run Scout and Echo in parallel when their inputs permit it.
- Run Guardian's calculations in deterministic code, not through repeated LLM calls.
- Limit proposal revisions and total model calls per session.
- Cache reusable analytics with explicit freshness rules.
- Reuse completed workflow stages when the inputs have not changed.
- Keep prompts and outputs compact; do not send entire chat histories by default.

Track these values for every session:

- Total model calls and token usage.
- Estimated AI cost.
- Session duration.
- Number of evidence records inspected.
- Validation and retry failures.
- Execution success and failure.
- Owner approval and rejection rates.
- Recommendations that resulted in measurable outcomes.

Set actual limits after benchmarking with your Gemini model and Edge Function runtime. Avoid assuming a fixed cost or latency before measuring it.

## 16. What makes Sale Brain defensible?

The differentiator is not simply having five AI agents. Another developer can reproduce that architecture.

The durable advantage is the combination of five things:

1\. Verified shop knowledge

Structured orders, products, stock, customer inquiries, delivery rules, and financial constraints.

2\. Decision history

What the owner approved, rejected, edited, and learned over time.

3\. An execution network

Direct connections to Telegram conversations, campaigns, orders, and outcomes.

4\. Financial and operational guardrails

Recommendations must respect verified costs, stock, policies, and owner approvals.

5\. Outcome evidence

Over time, Sale Brain can measure which recommendations work for which types of shops and under what conditions.

For Myanmar-focused commerce, make Burmese and English communication, township delivery pricing, local shop workflows, and reliable payment-status handling first-class capabilities. Payment claims must remain distinct from verified payments.

The long-term advantage comes from better evidence, better execution, and verified learning—not from claiming the AI has become smarter merely because it has more agents.

## 17. Product success metrics

Track the following from the first pilot.

| Metric                        | What it measures                                                    |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Evidence accuracy             | Whether material claims are supported by the correct source records |
| Financial correctness         | Whether calculations match independently computed results           |
| Recommendation acceptance     | Whether owners approve, edit, or reject proposals                   |
| Execution reliability         | Whether approved actions complete correctly without duplication     |
| Time saved                    | Reduction in owner effort compared with their existing workflow     |
| Conversion                    | Change in eligible inquiry-to-order conversion                      |
| Contribution                  | Change in contribution after relevant variable costs                |
| Incremental lift              | Improvement against a suitable control or baseline                  |
| AI cost per completed council | Financial sustainability of the feature                             |

Do not optimize only for recommendation acceptance. A model that tells owners what they want to hear may receive approvals without creating business value.

Likewise, don't claim that Sale Brain increases revenue by a particular percentage until controlled or otherwise credible measurement supports the claim.

## 20. Give your coding agent a clear development contract

Use the following as the persistent instruction for your coding agent before implementing the feature.

# Sale Brain Team — Engineering Contract

## Objective

Implement Sale Brain Team, an evidence-based virtual sales department, inside the existing Sale Brain repository. The feature must investigate real shop data, develop and compare strategies, protect profitability, obtain owner approval, execute approved actions safely, and measure outcomes.

## Before changing code

1. Inspect the repository, existing migrations, database schema, authentication, shop ownership model, AI services, Telegram integrations, Smart Marketing, and order lifecycle.
2. Produce a short architecture and schema-gap report.
3. Identify existing components and services to reuse.
4. Propose the first small implementation increment and its tests.
5. Do not recreate existing domain entities or replace working integrations without justification.

## Architecture

- Preserve the existing stack and project conventions.
- Keep business calculations deterministic and separate from LLM reasoning.
- Implement five bounded agent roles: Scout, Muse, Guardian, Echo, and Commander.
- Use structured, runtime-validated AI outputs.
- Persist sessions, agent runs, evidence, proposals, decisions, approvals, actions, and outcomes.
- Enforce tenant isolation and server-side authorization.
- Keep secrets out of frontend code.
- Make long-running work resumable and safe to retry.

## Evidence and financial integrity

- Every material factual claim must reference verifiable evidence or be explicitly labeled as an assumption or forecast.
- Include time ranges, data freshness, and data-quality limitations.
- Never invent metrics, historical trends, customer intent, or financial results.
- Never claim profitability when required cost data is missing.
- Calculate margins, opportunity scores, and policy limits in deterministic code.
- Preserve formula versions and the assumptions used in each decision.

## Approval and execution

- All external actions require explicit owner approval.
- An AI proposal is never directly executable.
- Validate the exact action payload before approval and again before execution.
- Bind approval to the approved payload version or hash.
- Reject stale, unauthorized, or policy-violating actions.
- Use idempotency keys and persistent execution records to prevent duplicates.
- Record execution results, failures, and audit history.
- Reuse the existing Telegram and marketing services.

## Business memory

- Store source references, verification status, timestamps, scope, and expiry.
- Separate owner policies from temporary observations.
- Let owners inspect, correct, and delete memory.
- Never convert unverified model-generated claims into verified facts.

## Development rules

- Work in small, reviewable increments.
- Write migrations that match the actual existing schema.
- Add unit tests for calculations and schemas.
- Add integration tests for authorization, session recovery, approvals, and execution.
- Test cross-shop access, prompt injection, missing data, stale prices, and duplicate requests.
- Do not hardcode sample metrics into production UI.
- Do not claim a feature is complete without running the relevant tests.
- Document unresolved assumptions and limitations.
- Prefer a smaller reliable implementation over an elaborate but unverified agent workflow.

## First deliverable

Complete the repository audit, map the existing data model and integrations, identify required schema changes, and implement the evidence/analytics foundation. Do not start by building all five agents at once.

My strongest recommendation: make Release 1 exceptionally reliable before adding autonomous execution. If Sale Brain can explain why a strategy is recommended, demonstrate that its numbers are correct, and show owners what happened after their decisions, you will have a much stronger product than a collection of agents that simply chat about sales.
