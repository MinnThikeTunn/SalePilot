# Feature Specification: AI Strategy Desk

## 1. Feature Overview

**Feature Name:** AI Strategy Desk  
**Product:** SalePilot AI  
**Category:** AI-Powered Business Analytics and Decision Support

### Purpose

Build an evidence-based AI Strategy Desk that helps small-business owners understand their sales performance, identify business opportunities, and decide what action to take next.

The feature must analyze actual business data, detect meaningful problems or opportunities, prioritize them using deterministic scoring rules, and use AI to explain the findings in clear, simple English.

The system must not behave as a generic chatbot or generate recommendations based on invented metrics.

### Core Promise

"Know what is happening in your business, understand why it matters, and know what to do next."

## 2. Primary User Story

As a small-business owner, I want SalePilot to analyze my sales, orders, products, inventory, and available customer-interaction data so that I can identify the most important actions to improve my business.

### Key Questions the Feature Must Answer

- How is my business performing?
- What has improved or declined?
- What is the most important problem or opportunity right now?
- What real data supports this recommendation?
- What should I do next?
- How reliable is this recommendation?
- How can I measure whether my action worked?

## 3. Functional Requirements

### FR-01: Business Data Analysis

The system must analyze available business data, including:

- Completed orders and revenue.
- Order counts and average order value.
- Sales trends across comparable periods.
- Product-level sales performance.
- Current inventory and stock coverage.
- Order cancellations.
- Customer inquiries and conversions, when tracking data exists.
- Product costs and contribution profit, when reliable cost data exists.

The system must use the existing database schema and business logic wherever possible.

Do not create duplicate versions of existing order, product, inventory, or shop data.

All financial calculations must be performed by deterministic backend code or validated database queries, not generated by an LLM.

### FR-02: Data Quality Validation

Before generating recommendations, validate the availability, freshness, completeness, and comparability of the required data.

The system must detect:

- Missing or stale inventory data.
- Missing product costs.
- Empty or insufficient historical periods.
- Zero-value comparison baselines.
- Incomplete order synchronization.
- Duplicate events.
- Unknown or inconsistent order statuses.
- Missing inquiry tracking.
- Incompatible currencies or timezones.

Every analysis must expose a data-quality status:

- `good`
- `limited`
- `insufficient`

When data is insufficient, the system must explain what is missing instead of fabricating a conclusion.

### FR-03: Opportunity Detection

Implement a modular, rule-based opportunity detection engine.

Initial supported opportunity types:

1. **Sales Decline:** Detect meaningful decreases in revenue or completed orders across comparable periods.
2. **Stock Risk:** Identify products whose estimated stock coverage is lower than their replenishment lead time or configured safety threshold.
3. **Cancellation Risk:** Detect meaningful cancellation-rate changes using a clearly defined denominator.
4. **Conversion Gap:** Identify weak inquiry-to-order conversion when reliable inquiry and order linkage exists.
5. **Unconverted Inquiries:** Identify eligible inquiries that have not converted within a defined window, when supported by available event data.
6. **Margin Opportunity:** Identify potential improvements in contribution profit when reliable cost and fee data exists.
7. **Repeat Purchase Opportunity:** Identify suitable repeat-purchase opportunities when reliable customer and order history exists.

Each detector must have its own eligibility conditions, metric calculations, evidence requirements, and tests.

Do not display a recommendation when its required data is unavailable.

### FR-04: Opportunity Scoring and Ranking

The backend must calculate a deterministic priority score between 0 and 100.

Initial configurable scoring weights:

- Potential business impact: 35%.
- Evidence strength: 25%.
- Urgency: 20%.
- Actionability: 20%.

Formula:

`priorityScore = impact × 0.35 + evidence × 0.25 + urgency × 0.20 + actionability × 0.20`

Each component must be normalized to a value between 0 and 100.

All weights, thresholds, and scoring rules must be configurable and versioned.

The scoring engine must:

- Apply eligibility checks before scoring.
- Suppress duplicate opportunities.
- Apply business constraints.
- Preserve previous owner decisions.
- Rank eligible opportunities.
- Limit the number of recommendations shown at once.
- Store the scoring version and component breakdown.

The LLM must never calculate, override, or modify the authoritative priority score.

The priority score represents recommendation priority, not the probability of success.

### FR-05: Evidence and Explainability

Every opportunity must include:

- Opportunity type and title.
- Description of the identified problem.
- Supporting metrics and values.
- Source of each metric.
- Analysis period and comparison period, where applicable.
- Recommended action.
- Reason the action is relevant.
- Priority score and score breakdown.
- Evidence confidence.
- Expected impact estimate, if calculable.
- Assumptions and limitations.
- Data-quality warnings.
- Whether owner approval is required.

Evidence must be structured and persisted so the UI can display it and the backend can reproduce the calculation.

Do not rely exclusively on free-text AI explanations as evidence.

### FR-06: AI-Generated Explanation

Use the existing Gemini integration where appropriate.

The AI layer must receive a verified analysis bundle containing calculated metrics, eligible opportunities, evidence, constraints, and quality warnings.

The AI must:

- Explain findings in clear, concise English.
- Describe why each recommendation matters.
- Explain the recommended next action.
- Communicate relevant limitations.
- Distinguish observed facts from estimates and hypotheses.
- Use only supplied metrics and evidence.
- Return structured JSON conforming to a validated schema.

The AI must not:

- Invent sales figures, costs, trends, or conversion rates.
- Claim unsupported percentage increases.
- Change scores, confidence labels, or calculated estimates.
- Recommend actions that violate supplied business constraints.
- Present correlation as proven causation.
- Claim that a recommendation has improved sales before results are measured.

Validate all AI responses on the backend. Reject malformed output, unsupported metric references, unknown opportunity IDs, and invalid action types.

### FR-07: Strategy Desk Dashboard

Provide a clear, owner-friendly interface containing:

1. **Business Overview**
   - Revenue and completed orders.
   - Relevant comparisons against a previous period.
   - Availability and freshness of the data.

2. **Top Priority**
   - The highest-ranked eligible opportunity.
   - A concise explanation of why it matters.
   - The recommended next action.

3. **Opportunity List**
   - Ranked recommendation cards.
   - Priority score.
   - Evidence summary.
   - Confidence label.
   - Important limitations.

4. **Evidence Details**
   - Supporting metrics.
   - Source period.
   - Comparison values.
   - Calculation details and warnings.

5. **Owner Actions**
   - Accept recommendation.
   - Dismiss recommendation.
   - Mark action as in progress.
   - Mark action as completed.
   - View recorded outcomes.

6. **Data Quality Notice**
   - Clearly explain missing or stale data.
   - Identify which recommendations are unavailable or limited because of data quality.

Use simple language suitable for non-technical business owners. Avoid overwhelming the user with technical metrics or long AI-generated reports.

All user-facing text, labels, explanations, and AI-generated content must be in English.

### FR-08: Recommendation Lifecycle

Support these opportunity states:

- `open`
- `accepted`
- `dismissed`
- `in_progress`
- `completed`
- `expired`

Record owner actions and timestamps.

Preserve historical opportunity snapshots and decisions across analysis runs.

Do not automatically reset a dismissed recommendation to open without a meaningful reason, such as newly detected evidence or a changed business condition.

### FR-09: Outcome Measurement

When an owner completes a recommended action, allow the system to record:

- Action completion time.
- Baseline metrics.
- Observation period.
- Observed metrics.
- Attribution method.
- Outcome notes.

Use actual sales and campaign data wherever available.

Distinguish measured outcomes from estimates. A before-and-after increase must not automatically be described as an effect caused by the recommendation.

### FR-10: Safe Failure Handling

If Gemini fails or returns invalid output:

- Keep valid deterministic analytics available.
- Use a simple template-based English explanation where possible.
- Mark the AI explanation as unavailable or using a fallback.
- Do not fabricate replacement metrics.
- Do not return fake uplift estimates.
- Log a useful internal error without exposing secrets or private customer data.

If analytics fail, return a clearly marked error or a previously cached result with its actual analysis timestamp and freshness status.

## 4. Data Model Requirements

Reuse existing tables wherever possible.

Introduce or extend data structures for:

- Analytics runs.
- Sales opportunities.
- Opportunity outcomes.
- Normalized sales events, if existing events cannot support the required analytics.
- Historical product costs, if profit analysis requires them.

### Analytics Run

Must record:

- Run ID.
- Shop ID.
- Analysis window.
- Comparison window or equivalent period metadata.
- Shop timezone and currency.
- Source-data watermark.
- Metric and scoring versions.
- Execution status.
- Data-quality summary.
- Warnings and errors.

### Sales Opportunity

Must record:

- Opportunity ID and shop ID.
- Analytics run ID.
- Stable opportunity key.
- Opportunity type.
- Structured evidence snapshot.
- Impact estimate and assumptions.
- Score breakdown.
- Priority score.
- Evidence confidence.
- Recommended action.
- Approval requirement.
- Lifecycle status.
- Detection and expiry timestamps.

### Opportunity Outcome

Must record:

- Opportunity ID and shop ID.
- Owner action.
- Baseline metrics.
- Observed metrics.
- Observation period.
- Attribution method.
- Notes and timestamps.

Use appropriate foreign keys, indexes, constraints, and tenant isolation policies.

## 5. Backend Architecture

Implement the feature using the existing project architecture and conventions.

Separate responsibilities into modules equivalent to:

- `metrics`: deterministic business calculations.
- `dataQuality`: validation and metric eligibility.
- `opportunityDetectors`: opportunity detection rules.
- `opportunityScoring`: scoring, deduplication, and ranking.
- `explanationService`: Gemini prompts and validated explanations.
- `persistence`: analytics runs, opportunities, and outcomes.
- `strategyService`: orchestration and caching.
- `validators`: request and response validation.

These are suggested responsibilities, not a requirement to duplicate existing services or create unnecessary files.

### Processing Flow

1. Authenticate the user.
2. Verify access to the requested shop.
3. Load data using tenant-scoped queries.
4. Validate data quality.
5. Calculate deterministic metrics.
6. Detect eligible opportunities.
7. Calculate scores and rank recommendations.
8. Persist the analysis run and evidence snapshots.
9. Generate AI explanations from the verified bundle.
10. Validate AI output.
11. Persist valid explanations and return the result.
12. Record owner decisions and later outcomes.

Never expose Supabase service-role credentials or Gemini API keys in frontend code.

## 6. API Requirements

Support equivalent operations through the project's existing routing conventions:

- Analyze business performance.
- Retrieve a specific analysis run.
- List active opportunities.
- Accept an opportunity.
- Dismiss an opportunity.
- Mark an opportunity as in progress or completed.
- Record an opportunity outcome.

Validate all inputs and authorization on the backend. Never trust a client-provided shop ID without verifying access.

Use typed request and response contracts and consistent error handling.

## 7. Caching and Data Freshness

Cache results only when the cache key accounts for the shop, analysis window, metric version, scoring version, and relevant source-data changes.

Invalidate or refresh cached results when relevant orders, products, stock, costs, or events change.

Return the actual source watermark and analysis timestamp. Do not label old cached results as current.

## 8. Security and Privacy

- Enforce tenant isolation through Supabase RLS and backend authorization.
- Apply appropriate read/write policies to all new tables.
- Keep customer personal information out of aggregate analytics and AI prompts unless explicitly required and authorized.
- Use pseudonymous customer identifiers for event linkage.
- Validate ownership of every opportunity before changing its status.
- Never expose secrets, raw payment information, or private conversation content in logs.
- Make actions that send messages, launch campaigns, or change prices require explicit owner approval.

## 9. Testing Requirements

Create unit tests for metric calculations, data-quality rules, opportunity detectors, and scoring.

Create integration tests for database access, tenant isolation, opportunity persistence, lifecycle transitions, and safe fallback behavior.

Required test cases include:

- Revenue decline calculated correctly.
- Zero baseline produces an unavailable percentage change.
- Missing cost prevents unsupported profit recommendations.
- Stale stock data triggers the appropriate warning.
- Duplicate events are not counted twice.
- Missing inquiry data prevents conversion claims.
- Invalid AI JSON is rejected safely.
- AI-generated unsupported metrics are rejected.
- Cross-shop data access is denied.
- Previous owner decisions are preserved across runs.
- Failed AI calls do not fabricate analytics.
- Empty datasets return an informative state.

All financial metrics and score calculations must be testable without calling an LLM.

## 10. Implementation Rules for Coding Agents

Before changing code:

1. Inspect the repository structure and existing Strategy Desk implementation.
2. Inspect existing database tables, migrations, types, order statuses, and RLS policies.
3. Inspect the current AI Strategy Edge Function and fallback behavior.
4. Identify existing analytics and shared services that can be reused.
5. Document schema mismatches and propose the smallest compatible changes.

During implementation:

- Preserve existing working functionality.
- Avoid unrelated refactoring.
- Do not create duplicate business entities or redundant API layers.
- Do not assume proposed table or column names already exist.
- Use migrations for database changes.
- Keep business calculations deterministic and independently testable.
- Keep the LLM limited to explanation and language generation.
- Do not hardcode fabricated historical chart values, sales peaks, or expected uplift.
- Keep all user-facing content in English.
- Update relevant types, tests, and documentation.

After implementation:

1. Run relevant tests and type checks.
2. Verify tenant isolation and RLS behavior.
3. Verify analytics against known test data.
4. Verify AI schema validation and fallback behavior.
5. Report files changed, migrations added, tests executed, and remaining limitations.
6. Clearly distinguish implemented functionality from planned or unverified behavior.

## 11. Definition of Done

The feature is complete when:

- The Strategy Desk uses real, validated business data.
- Revenue and other metrics can be reproduced from source records.
- Unsupported metrics are suppressed rather than invented.
- Eligible opportunities are detected and ranked deterministically.
- Each recommendation has traceable evidence and clear limitations.
- AI explanations follow the validated English-only output contract.
- Scores and verified financial values cannot be changed by the LLM.
- Owners can accept, dismiss, and complete recommendations.
- Analysis runs and decisions are persisted.
- Data-quality status and freshness are visible.
- Tenant access is properly enforced.
- Tests cover normal, edge, and failure cases.
- Existing SalePilot functionality remains intact.

**Engineering principle:** Code determines what is true, business rules determine what is actionable, and AI explains why it matters.
